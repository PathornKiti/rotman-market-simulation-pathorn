# Verify the bell fine before tuning anything else

**Risk flag first: the bot's hold-to-the-bell plan may be fined $1.00 or more for every share still open at the end of the heat, and nobody has read a practice Transaction Log to rule it out.** Rotman's stock Liability Trading casefile has a fourth fine parameter, "the fine rate for shares not covered by the end of the case iteration", set to $1.00/share by default, and RITC 2026 used $10/share. The 2027 brief copies the stock casefile's first two tiers word for word. At the end-of-heat inventories the simulator produces (up to ~125k shares), a $1 fine wipes out a heat, while the hold itself is worth only ~$11-15k simulated. So until a practice heat shows zero fines at the bell, the graded-heat default should be a fine-proof end-game. Beyond that flag, outside research leaves room to improve, but mostly in **mechanics we have not verified** rather than in new models. The largest documented gain is one the repo has already measured and switched off: answering tenders 3 ticks before expiry (`decide_late_ticks = 3`, +$17-25k/heat, t 3.1-4.5 in our simulator), which is also what every competitor bot found does for auctions. Next comes releasing the net-limit room we hold for pending auction bids, if RIT turns out to void awards that no longer fit. The academic material (knapsack bid prices, risk-bid pricing, GLFT/Obizhaeva-Wang execution) mostly confirms that our design is already near the theoretical optimum. Several of its headline proposals match ideas we have already tested and rejected, so the report lists them under "do not pursue".

## An uncovered-share fine could erase a heat at the bell

Rotman's instructor notes for LT3, the classroom version of this case, describe a `LIABILITY_RISK` fines module with four parameters: a speculation fine up to a share threshold, the threshold, a higher fine above it, and "Parameter 4 sets the fine rate for shares not covered by the end of the case iteration … The values for these parameters are set at **$0.20, 5000, $0.40 and $1.00**" ([LT3 SOLUTION brief](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RIT%20-%20Case%20Brief%20-%20LT3%20-%20Dynamic%20Order%20Arrival%20-%20SOLUTION.pdf)). RITC 2026 set that fourth parameter to **$10 per uncovered share**, applied "real-time in RIT" ([RITC 2026 Case Package](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RITC%202026%20Case%20Package.pdf)). Our 2027 brief lists only $0.20 for the first 5,000 shares and $0.40 beyond, which are exactly the stock defaults for parameters 1-3, and it does not mention parameter 4. That pattern suggests the organisers started from the stock casefile and edited it: they have already raised tier 3 to $0.60. Nothing in it shows that parameter 4 was set to zero. A second, independent problem: the official student brief defines speculative trading as including "**any nonzero positions at the end of the trading period**" ([LT3 SOLUTION brief](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RIT%20-%20Case%20Brief%20-%20LT3%20-%20Dynamic%20Order%20Arrival%20-%20SOLUTION.pdf)). So even with parameter 4 at zero, shares open at the bell could be charged at the $0.60 speculation rate.

The break-even arithmetic is lopsided. Hold-to-the-bell (`close_hold_ticks = 90`, `hold_risk_budget = 40000`, `hold_valuation`) is worth about $11-15k/heat in our simulator (`docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md`, A15). A $1 fine on 50k open shares costs $50k, so holding pays only if the chance of a fine is below roughly 22-30%. At 125k open shares the break-even falls to 9-12%. At the 2026 rate of $10 it falls to 1-3%. Rank scoring makes this worse: RITC 2026 ranks teams within each sub-heat and was "designed to discourage participants from 'betting the house'" ([RITC 2026 Case Package](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RITC%202026%20Case%20Package.pdf)), so one fined heat costs a whole rank, and the next heat's gain does not buy it back. **Until verified, run graded heats with the documented A15 fallback** (`hold_risk_budget=0`, `close_hold_ticks=0`, `hold_valuation=false`, `min_ticks_to_unwind=15`), or with the cheaper flat-by-the-bell variant tested in simulator candidate 2 below. Our earlier practice heats ran this hold, but nobody read their Transaction Logs (`docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md` §6 q1). If RIT's end-of-iteration report or Trader Info "Total Fines" from those heats can still be opened, that may answer the question with no extra heat. Rotman says fines appear in the transaction log, a "Case Fines Summary" and "Total Fines" ([LT3 SOLUTION brief](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RIT%20-%20Case%20Brief%20-%20LT3%20-%20Dynamic%20Order%20Arrival%20-%20SOLUTION.pdf)).

One fact from this run matters more than any of the numbers. At the real volatility (~$0.035/s), a fresh 55k

never crosses: the "flat within 30 s" schedule is effectively off. That is fine while the close is free and prices

two slow real unwinds (701 / 893 shares/s with $0.83 / $1.76 adverse moves) put in doubt, and the simulator cannot
test the second. So the cheap protection is the one already ranked first: check 1 at the next practice heat, then
flat-by-the-bell if a fine exists.

## Eight things the next practice heat must settle

The RIT API returns only `{success: bool}` on a tender POST and does not document re-bidding, award timing, or what happens when a won block no longer fits the limits ([REST swagger](https://rit.306w.ca/RIT-REST-API/1.0.3/swagger.yaml)). ## Five simulator tests, ranked by expected effect

The ranking below weighs expected dollars per heat, how directly outside evidence supports the mechanism, and how likely the change is to pass our rule (paired t ≥ 2 on design seeds and on a fresh holdout, in calibrated, default and hostile markets). Seeds 1-16, 101-116, 201-216, 301-316 and 401-432 have already been used, so each test below should take a new holdout block (for example 501-516). Use `late_award=1` throughout, because that is how real awards book.

| Rank | Change | Expected effect | Depends on practice check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Re-enable late answering (`decide_late_ticks = 3`), re-measured on the current config | +$17-25k/heat on the old default simulator, but only +$4k (t 0.9, not significant) on the practice-calibrated market (2026-10-12, docs/PRACTICE_LOGS.md sec. 5): treat as uncertain, possibly small | #5 |
| 2 | Fine-proof end-game: flat by the bell vs the A15 fallback | Saves $50k+ per heat if a fine exists; costs ≤ $11-15k if not | #1, #8 |
| 3 | Release the net room held for pending auction bids | +$2-6k/heat, minus what `room_wait` already rescues | #2 |
| 4 | Absorption-aware hold/cross budget (GLFT threshold) | Mean about $0 ± 2k; smaller worst-heat and CVaR losses | none |
| 5 | Per-slot reserve bracket for competitive auctions | +$0-3k/heat, only if reserves are fixed per slot | #3, #4 |

**1. Re-validate late answering on today's config.** The repo already measured `decide_late_ticks = 3` at +$25.3k (t 4.5) on seeds 301-316, +$17.0k (t 3.1) on 1-16 and +$22.0k (t 3.5) on holdout 101-116, and the worst stressed heat improved from −$104.9k to −$2.5k (`config/liability.toml`). It is currently 0 by user choice for practice. Outside practice points the same way. Every reviewed auction bot submits at `expires-1` or `expires-2` ([Ray.py](https://github.com/raye92/ritc-trading/blob/main/competition2/Liquidity/Ray.py); [Felix Poirier](https://github.com/felixpoirier1/RIT/blob/main/Liquidity/main.py)). raye92 re-checks private tenders every tick until expiry, and the Babson tutorial says to use the 30 s window to wait for the book to improve ([Babson LT4 tutorial](https://www.babson.edu/media/babson/assets/cutler-center/Rotman-Trading_LT4-Case-Tutorial_Spinnell_FINAL.pdf)). A further mechanism is our own inference and is not in any source: the award is decided at expiry, so an auction bid fixed at arrival wins mostly when the mid has since moved against it, which is a staleness version of the winner's curse. Repricing at `expires-3` removes it. The earlier measurement predates the hold, `room_wait` and the order-settle fix, so the test is: current config vs `--set strategy.decide_late_ticks=3`, 16 design seeds plus the new holdout, calibrated, default and `--hostile`, with `RITC_STRESS=late_award=1`. Repeat with `reserve_lo=0.15,reserve_hi=0.40` so that auctions are actually won. This is the cheapest test. Its size is uncertain: on the practice-calibrated market (`RITC_STRESS=real=1`) answering late measured only +$4k (t 0.9) on 2026-10-12, far below the older +$17-25k, so the calibrated market decides.

**2. Make the end-game fine-proof at the lowest cost.** The simulator can already approximate a bell fine: `close_slip` moves the closing price against whoever holds at the bell by $X/share, which has the same P&L effect as a per-share fine. Compare three arms: (a) the current hold; (b) the A15 fallback flags; (c) a new flat-by-the-bell variant that keeps `hold_risk_budget` resting during the heat but replaces the last-90-s passive hold with an Almgren-Chriss wind-down that reaches zero by T−5 s, timed to use the end-of-case market-maker liquidity Rotman describes. Run each under `close_slip` = 0, 0.2, 0.6 and 1.0. The decision rule is not the usual one. Pick whichever of (b) or (c) loses least against (a) at `close_slip=0` and beats (a) at 0.2 and above. Our simulator does not model the end-of-case depth boost, so it overstates the cost of flattening, which makes it a conservative test. Theory explains why this choice is forced rather than optional. With a free terminal close and no risk aversion, the optimal unwind speed is zero ([GLFT 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.3279)). Once a terminal penalty exists, the same models trade toward completion by T ([Obizhaeva & Wang](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11444)). Our bell hold is correct only if the close is truly free.

**3. Free the room held by pending auction bids (only if check 2 shows voided awards).** The bot currently counts a pending auction bid as if it had already filled. That blocked a fixed tender in 2 of 5 real heats, about $15k each time, once for an auction we went on to lose (`docs/PRACTICE_LOGS.md`). If RIT voids an award that no longer fits, a fixed tender taken during the auction window costs nothing, and the auction bid becomes a free option that fills only if room is left at expiry. Revenue-management theory supports the same move: in Talluri and van Ryzin's bid-price control, the right reservation is the opportunity cost of the capacity a request uses ([Talluri & van Ryzin 1998](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/3933/network_revenue_management.pdf)). If the award is voided, an auction bid uses no capacity until it actually fills. The reusable-resource literature also counts capacity that "returns soon" as available ([Goyal, Iyengar & Udwani](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.02430)), which is the logic `room_wait` already uses. The simulator checks limits at bid time and drops the tender after one POST (`src/ritc/sim/server.py`), so this needs a small change: with `late_award=1`, add an `award_room` option that re-checks `_over_limit` at expiry and voids the award. Add a second mode that delivers the award and applies the over-limit fine, to bound the downside if the real server behaves that way. The arms are the current room reservation vs no reservation for pending bids. Use a stress where the effect can appear: tenders ×2 and generous reserves (`reserve_lo=0.15,reserve_hi=0.40`), plus calibrated, default and hostile markets. Expect +$2-6k/heat at most, less what `room_wait` (+$4.9k/heat) already recovers.

**4. Cross on falling absorption, not on the clock.** The two real slow unwinds absorbed 701 and 893 shares/s, against 2,500-3,600 normally, and came with adverse moves of $1.76 and $0.83/share (`docs/PRACTICE_LOGS.md`). In GLFT's optimal liquidation model, the long-horizon optimal ask turns negative ("send a market order") once inventory exceeds a threshold that scales with √(fill rate)/σ ([GLFT 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.3279)). Guéant's risk-liquidity premium for a constant-rate unwind is inversely proportional to the absorption rate ([Guéant, arXiv 1210.7608](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.7608)). Both point to a direction-free rule: replace the `hold_risk_budget` sd, |q|·σ·√(ticks left), with the passive-unwind sd σ·√(q³/(3·Â)) whenever Â·ticks_left > q, where Â is an EWMA of our own resting fills over the last 5-10 s. Lo, MacKinlay and Zhang found that hypothetical fills built from the tape are "very poor proxies" for real ones, so Â must come from our own orders ([Lo, MacKinlay & Zhang](https://web.mit.edu/Alo/www/Papers/limit10.html)). Under a pure random walk this cannot raise the mean; it only trades cost for tails. So use the repo's existing tail rule: mean not worse than t −1, with worst-heat and CVaR clearly better. Stresses: `flow=0.3`; `kyle=0.3,kyle_phi=0.9`, which models one-sided flow that both starves our resting side and moves the price; and the hostile market. If the mean rises under `kyle`, attribute the gain to that assumed coupling, not to the rule.

**5. Bracket the competitive-auction reserve per slot (only after checks 3 and 4).** For a competitive auction, our fill is at our own price whenever it beats the reserve. Against a fixed reserve the profit-maximising bid is therefore the reserve plus one tick, whatever our value estimate. Kleinberg and Leighton's staircase learns a fixed unknown threshold from accept/reject feedback with regret Θ(log log n), the best possible ([Kleinberg & Leighton](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/%7Erdk/papers/oppa.pdf)). It uses only the order of outcomes, so it avoids the tail-shape sensitivity that sank our censored-normal plus Myerson learner. Tender ids are fixed slots: 49+800k in a 2026 class casefile ([Columbia tender log](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/HEAD/LT3/data%20and%20logs%20(from%20the%20competition)/lt3_competition_tenders.csv)), and 3+100k plus 700-703 on our server. Together with both wins coming on id 702, that makes a per-slot reserve plausible. The rule: per slot and caption type, keep the most aggressive losing offset and the least aggressive winning offset. Step toward the mid only after a loss, and never past the value estimate minus the existing margin. Switch it off for good the first time a win lands below an earlier losing offset, because that means reserves are random, and with random reserves learning costs at least √n regret. Testing it needs two simulator changes: a `reserve_mode=slot` option (a fixed offset per slot id) alongside the current i.i.d. U(lo, hi), and multi-heat sequences (e.g. 5 heats per seed) so the learner's state carries over. Keep it only if it wins under `slot` reserves and is neutral (|t| < 1) under i.i.d. reserves in the hostile market. This is ranked last because "bid closer to mid" (`competitive_margin` 0.02/0.03) already lost $3.3-3.8k in the hostile market. The cause was that a near-reserve win is a ~$0.10/share trade that takes half the net limit (`docs/RESEARCH.md`). A bracket only moves toward the mid where the evidence says it must. For winner-take-all slots, the literature says to do the opposite of chasing. The winner is the team with the most optimistic estimate, and the correction is about one full estimate-error half-width ([Kagel & Levin survey](https://www.asc.ohio-state.edu/kagel.4/CVsurvey.short.PDF)). So keep the current conservative winner-take-all bid.

Two low-value refinements can wait. The first is resting 1-2 ticks behind the touch once little inventory is left, as GLFT's monotone depth implies ([GLFT 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1106.3279)). Fill probabilities beyond the best quote are "typically negligible" ([Lokin & Yu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02572)), so the gain is a few hundred dollars at most. The second is sizing catch-up sweeps by the measured refill rate instead of 35% of visible depth. Obizhaeva and Wang show execution cost becomes strategy-independent when refill is very fast or very slow ([Obizhaeva & Wang](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11444)), and Guéant bounds the gain of an optimised schedule over constant participation at about 14% ([Guéant](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.7608)). Schedule shape is second-order.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the remaining edge is. Theory and past teams agree that the bot's core economics are sound. Pricing the block against walkable liquidity, resting rather than crossing, and accepting at a thin margin are what Rotman's own solution, Guéant's block pricing and the GLFT execution results recommend. Upstairs-block evidence that true costs are only 20-35% of a displayed-book walk ([Bessembinder & Venkataraman](https://scholar.smu.edu/business_finance_research/41)) explains our measured $0.19/share pessimism without requiring a new model. What remains is mostly about rules and timing on the real server: whether the bell is fined, whether an award needs room at expiry, whether a bid can be revised, and whether we answer late. Each one is a single observation in a practice heat. Each can be worth more than any modelling refinement on the list.

The practical order for the next session follows from that. Check the old heats' fine reports, then run one practice heat that executes checks 1-5 deliberately, and put the A15 fallback (or the cheaper candidate 2 variant) in the graded-heat config until check 1 clears. In parallel, re-run the late-answering A/B on the current config, because it is the only candidate whose expected effect is already measured in five figures. Everything that requires predicting price direction or pricing room more tightly has either failed our tests or is shown by theory to be worth cents. Further effort there would repeat rejected work.
