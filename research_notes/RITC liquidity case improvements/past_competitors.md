# Past competitors and official material on the RITC Liquidity Risk / Liability Trading (LT3) case

Scope note: public material on this case is thin. It amounts to one detailed post-mortem (Columbia, Sep 2026, classroom LT3), one practitioner tutorial (Babson LT4, c. 2019-20), about seven competitor code repos (RPI 2023, Felix Poirier 2023, U Calgary 2026, raye92 RITC 2026, lorenzourera LT3, Helen LT4, CMU 2024 stub), and the official FRTL briefs and packages (LT3 + SOLUTION instructor notes, RITCx Liability Trading, RITC 2019/2020/2023/2026 packages, RITC 2026 Closing Thoughts). Searches of Medium, LinkedIn, Reddit and YouTube found no write-ups of this case. Quantnet's Baruch "wins RITC" threads (2021, 2023, 2024) exist but return HTTP 403 to automated fetches, so their content could not be checked. All figures below were read from the primary files. Our bot's context: 420 s heats, about 11 tenders, CRZY/TAME/CROC, $0.02 fee, 100k/250k limits, no fine at close-out.

## Q1. What acceptance thresholds, unwind styles and auction/WTA bidding rules did teams use, and what scores did they report?

### Takeaway
Every competitor bot found unwinds with **market orders in max-size chunks, starting right after booking**: the whole block goes in less than 1 s, or a capped 2k-20k shares per tick. Acceptance hurdles range from "full-book sweep VWAP beats the tender by at least $0.05/sh after fees" (Columbia) to "touch beats tender by fee + $0.04-$0.50 volatility buffer" (RITC 2026 bots). Auction/WTA bids are almost always **submitted 1-2 ticks before expiry**, priced at touch ∓ (fee + buffer). Two practitioners report numbers: Columbia made $70k over 7 class heats, with +$10.1k in a heat whose class range was -$136.6k to +$105.6k. Babson LT4 reports practice heats of $40-110k, with about $75k called "near-perfect" average play.

### Cited Findings
**Columbia (Nathaniel Coulter, Sep 2026, classroom LT3: 5 tenders per 300 s, CRZY/TAME, 65-75k-share blocks, $0.02 fee, 100k/250k limits)**
- Acceptance rule: E = full-block sweep VWAP − tender price − $0.02 fee − $0.03 safety reserve. V1-V4 required E ≥ $0.03 (about a 6¢ hurdle after fees). V5 cut this to E ≥ $0.02 (a 5¢ core hurdle), plus a minimum modelled profit of $750 and a requirement that the visible book covers the full block. A "marginal" 4-5¢ regime was allowed only when favourable full-depth book imbalance was ≥ +0.05. — [Coulter post-mortem PDF](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Nathaniel%20Coulter%20lt3_postmortem_report.pdf)
- Lowering the hurdle from 6¢ to 5¢ added 4 trades worth +$12,533 realized on the same path. One of the four lost $325: it arrived with 5.15¢ of edge, which fell by 5.64¢ during booking. — [same PDF, §2.6](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Nathaniel%20Coulter%20lt3_postmortem_report.pdf)
- Unwind: once the position was booked, the whole block was **pipelined as max-size market orders** (25k CRZY / 10k TAME). Averages in class: burst 56.5 ms, back to flat 259.6 ms after booking. Execution vs the refreshed book added only +$357 in total, and 54 of 55 calibration slices filled at exactly the predicted sweep VWAP. — [same PDF, §2.3, §4.5](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Nathaniel%20Coulter%20lt3_postmortem_report.pdf)
- Results by phase. Demo/practice: V4.1 accepted 76 of 163 tenders, all 76 profitable, $709,829 realized. V5 accepted 25 of 34 for $187,118. V6 accepted 20 of 45 for $189,287. Class competition: 8 of 35 accepted (22.9%), $70,184.88 total over 7 heats. Best heats were $20,148 (2 tenders) and $18,426 (2 tenders). Two heats were $0 because nothing qualified. — [same PDF, Appendix A, §4.1](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Nathaniel%20Coulter%20lt3_postmortem_report.pdf)
- Class context from the official RIT report for Heat 2 (32 traders): Columbia made $10,122.76. The class average was $3,545.72, the median $5,761.91, and the **range −$136,592 to +$105,596**. — [same PDF, §4.6](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Nathaniel%20Coulter%20lt3_postmortem_report.pdf)
- The author's own reading: algorithms were disadvantaged at 5 tenders per heat because a human can inspect each one. If winning were the only goal, he says, practising manual trading might have been better. He says he cannot infer what higher-scoring classmates did, but that any strategy beating his had to rely on "later price movement, later liquidity, passive fills, or another forecast". — [same PDF, §5, §8](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Nathaniel%20Coulter%20lt3_postmortem_report.pdf)
- Tender pricing in his class log: across 35 tenders, the median premium to mid was $0.225/sh for CRZY (range 0.01-0.28) and $0.197/sh for TAME (range −0.04 to 0.31), on blocks of 65-75k shares. (Computed by us from his `lt3_competition_tenders.csv`.) — [tender log CSV](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/LT3/data%20and%20logs%20(from%20the%20competition)/lt3_competition_tenders.csv)

**Babson LT4 tutorial (Alexander Spinnell, Babson '20; LT4 = CRZY/TAME with a main and an alternative market; manual trading)**
- Acceptance: take sell tenders only if they are at least $0.20 above the last price. In thin books, ask for $0.40 or more ("I would not take any long position" below that). A model rule: accept if projected profit is above $5,000, where unwind cost is the VWAP of **only 60-70% of the block** against the book (an implicit refill assumption). If the edge is short, wait out the 30 s window to see whether it improves. — [Babson LT4 tutorial PDF](https://www.babson.edu/media/babson/assets/cutler-center/Rotman-Trading_LT4-Case-Tutorial_Spinnell_FINAL.pdf)
- Unwind: aim to be flat in **10-20 s**. Right after accepting, post 5-8 limit orders one cent inside the touch, then market orders. If none of the limits fill within 5 s, dump about 70k shares in the next 3-5 s and accept breakeven or a ~$10k loss "to avoid losing up to 50k". With a huge-edge tender, market the whole block at once to lock in the profit. — [same](https://www.babson.edu/media/babson/assets/cutler-center/Rotman-Trading_LT4-Case-Tutorial_Spinnell_FINAL.pdf)
- Scores: market orders alone give $40-50k per case "consistently". The author's showcased run made about $110k. "Near-perfect" average play is about $75k. Suggested target distribution over 10 runs: one of $100k+, two or three of $80-100k, two or three of $50-80k, two of $30-50k, one of $25-30k. A strong tender showed about $28k of instant P&L, of which about $20k was realized after impact. One exceptional tender showed about $60k instant and $40k realized, and "could solely determine the success for an entire iteration". — [same](https://www.babson.edu/media/babson/assets/cutler-center/Rotman-Trading_LT4-Case-Tutorial_Spinnell_FINAL.pdf)

**Official grading guideline (LT3 instructor notes, 5 tenders / 5 min)**: profit above $50k is "Very Good", above $35k "Good", above $10k "Average", above $0 "Needs improvement". — [RIT LT3 SOLUTION brief](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RIT%20-%20Case%20Brief%20-%20LT3%20-%20Dynamic%20Order%20Arrival%20-%20SOLUTION.pdf)

**RITC 2026 competitor bots (raye92/ritc-trading, `competition2/Liquidity`)**
- `Ray.py`, private tenders: cost = commission + liquidity EV (Low 0.05 / Med 0.03 / High 0.00) + volatility EV (Low 0.00 / Med 0.14 / High 0.30). An async task polls **every tick until expiry** and accepts the first tick on which the touch edge (bid − tender for a buy, tender − ask for a sell) exceeds cost. — [Ray.py](https://github.com/raye92/ritc-trading/blob/main/competition2/Liquidity/Ray.py)
- `Ray.py`, auctions/WTA: waits until `tick ≥ expires−1`, then bids at best bid − cost − $0.02 (to buy) or best ask + cost + $0.02 (to sell). — [Ray.py](https://github.com/raye92/ritc-trading/blob/main/competition2/Liquidity/Ray.py)
- `Ray.py`, unwind: every tick it sends market orders for min(position, 2k / 5k / 10k by liquidity class Low/Med/High). That is a fixed-rate "market-out" unwind of roughly 5-25 s for a 50k block. — [Ray.py](https://github.com/raye92/ritc-trading/blob/main/competition2/Liquidity/Ray.py)
- `Declan.py`: volatility buffers of 0.04 / 0.14 / 0.50. Accepts a private tender only after **3 consecutive ticks** of touch edge > fee + buffer. Auction/WTA bids are sent when `expires − tick < 2`, at touch ∓ (fee + buffer + $0.05). Unwind uses market orders capped per tick (5k Low/Med, 20k High). — [Declan.py](https://github.com/raye92/ritc-trading/blob/main/competition2/Liquidity/Declan.py)

**University of Calgary 2026 (manual Tk GUI, "LT3 Tender Tool")**: shows touch P&L = (bid − tender − fee)·Q. For auctions/WTA it pre-fills the bid at **best bid − fee − $0.01** (to buy) or best ask + fee + $0.01 (to sell), i.e. one tick plus the fee outside the touch. "Exit position" is a single click that sends market orders in 10k chunks. U Calgary placed 3rd overall at RITC 2023 and 2024. (Their liquidity-case rank is not published, and the 2026 result was not found.) — [Liquidity Trading Tool.py](https://github.com/UofCRylan/RITC_2026/blob/main/RITC-2026---University-of-Calgary-main/Liquidity%20Risk/Liquidity%20Trading%20Tool.py); [UCalgary RITC results](https://haskayne.ucalgary.ca/labs/finance-trading/trading-software/competitions/ritc-results)

**RPI 2023 (decision support; a human executes)**
- Private tenders, normal regime: accept if the price of the **deepest bid level needed to absorb the whole block** (the marginal level, not the VWAP) beats the tender price. That is more conservative than a VWAP rule. In the last 60 s it switches to a full-sweep VWAP. — [Main_Liquidity.py](https://github.com/JackMansfield2019/2023-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Liquidity_Case/Main_Liquidity.py)
- A spread filter applies: if the relative spread is ≥ 0.15%, it accepts only when SMA5 > SMA20 for longs (or < for shorts). That is a trend filter, i.e. speculation. — [same](https://github.com/JackMansfield2019/2023-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Liquidity_Case/Main_Liquidity.py)
- Auctions are evaluated only when fewer than 15 ticks remain in the window. The bid is the marginal level price for the full block. Recommended unwind: max-size orders, either at market ("MKT") or as a limit at that marginal price. — [same](https://github.com/JackMansfield2019/2023-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Liquidity_Case/Main_Liquidity.py)

**Felix Poirier, RITC 2023 (`Liquidity/main.py`)**: auto-accepts private tenders when they are profitable against a book-walk VWAP with slack = 0.1% of price. Auction bid = full-block book-walk VWAP × (1 ∓ 0.001), **submitted only at `tick == expiration−1`**. The unwind is manual: the script beeps and the human clicks. — [main.py](https://github.com/felixpoirier1/RIT/blob/main/Liquidity/main.py)

**Other LT3 tools**
- lorenzourera: WAC (book-walk) rule. Accept if the full quantity is sourced and WAC beats the tender. "Neutral" if WAC is favourable but depth is short. Advisory only. — [README](https://github.com/lorenzourera/rotman-liability-trading/blob/main/README.md)
- Helen (LT4): market orders only. Requires 10% price headroom at the full volume (`SAFE_BUFFER = 1.1`). Comments argue a tender never needs to be declined explicitly: hold it and re-check every tick until the window lapses. — [LT4 Liability Agency Trading.py](https://github.com/Helenbzbz/Quantitative-Trading-Strategies/blob/main/LT4%20Liability%20Agency%20Trading.py)

### Inferences
- (Inferred) Our bot accepts at ≥ $0.01/sh of book-walk edge (after drift and 0.3σ√T), which is already more aggressive than any competitor rule found: Columbia uses 5¢ plus a 3¢ reserve, RITC 2026 bots use fee + 4-50¢ against the touch, Babson uses 20-40¢. **Acceptance looseness is not where the gap is.**
- (Inferred) Our unwind is the most passive found: an iceberg at the touch, crossing only when behind an Almgren-Chriss schedule over about 30 s, then holding through the last 90 s. Every competitor goes to market at once (Columbia in under 1 s; raye92 at 2-20k/tick; Babson in 10-20 s with a 5 s stop on unfilled limits). Columbia's data shows market-order fills matched the book exactly, and booking-period moves were on average favourable (+$12.7k over 8 tenders). In that setup, fast crossing captured the arrival edge without paying for waiting.
- (Inferred) Auction bids. Ours sit at break-even − $0.05, about $0.24 from mid, and won 2 of 18. Competitors bid at touch ∓ (fee + 0-0.30 + 0.01-0.05), i.e. about 1-5¢ plus volatility buffer outside the touch. On low-volatility names that is as little as ~3-4¢ beyond the touch, much closer than our ~$0.24 from mid. Our "closer to mid" test was rejected in our simulator, so the real reserve level is the open question. None of the repos record their win rate.
- (Inferred) A $150-200k heat cannot come from spread capture alone. With ~11 tenders × ~55k shares and an LT3-like premium of ~$0.20-0.25/sh to mid (Columbia's class median), total premium on offer is about $120-150k **if every tender were accepted and unwound at mid**. The leader's ~$200k therefore most likely includes (a) favourable price drift on inventory held open (Columbia's class max was +$105k in a 5-tender heat, well above the ~5×70k×$0.22 ≈ $77k premium available, with the class min at −$137k), and/or (b) auction/WTA wins at prices far from mid.

### Gaps
- No public source reports **per-heat** RITC Liquidity Risk scores for top teams. Baruch MFE won this case in 2021 (1st and 2nd) and 2024 (per search snippets of [quantnet 2021](https://quantnet.com/threads/baruch-mfe-wins-1st-and-2nd-place-at-ritc-2021-5th-win-overall.47194/) and [quantnet 2024](https://quantnet.com/threads/baruch-mfe-ritc-2024-5th-win-in-a-row-8th-win-overall-all-time-scoring-record.57319/)), but the pages returned 403, so no strategy detail or P&L could be verified.
- None of the repos log auction win rates or reserve levels.
- No write-up found from a team that described exactly how it made $100k+ in one heat.

## Q2. Did anyone exploit the end-of-case close, holding inventory, acceptance timing, offsetting tenders, or the tender UI?

### Takeaway
Holding inventory on purpose is documented only by Babson LT4 (hold "around half", ~30k shares, when the book is thin on the opposing side; it added ~$20k in one run). The rules cut both ways. RITC 2023 scored "Adjusted P&L", where speculative profits were excluded and speculative losses counted. RITC 2026 fined **$10/share for shares still uncovered at the end**, with market makers adding liquidity into the close. Acceptance timing (wait within the window for the book to improve) and late auction submission are common. Offsetting a new tender against existing inventory is implemented in RPI 2023.

### Cited Findings
- **Holding with the trend (manual, LT4)**: "The first tender I held on to and followed the trend… made me an extra $20,000". Do this only when there is extremely low liquidity on the side that favours your position, hold only about half (~30k shares), and "rarely ever" hold a partial position when trading against others. — [Babson LT4 tutorial](https://www.babson.edu/media/babson/assets/cutler-center/Rotman-Trading_LT4-Case-Tutorial_Spinnell_FINAL.pdf)
- **Thin-book asymmetry is used as a directional cue**: when the bid side is very thin, "the probability of TAME going down $1 is significantly higher than going up $1". The response is to demand 40¢+ before going long. — [same](https://www.babson.edu/media/babson/assets/cutler-center/Rotman-Trading_LT4-Case-Tutorial_Spinnell_FINAL.pdf)
- **Trap orders**: resting orders far from the touch at the start of each run, to catch other traders' market orders. Effective "only when there are no trading fines for profiting from not having a position", and best early in the case or when liquidity is low. Others will undercut by a cent or two. — [same](https://www.babson.edu/media/babson/assets/cutler-center/Rotman-Trading_LT4-Case-Tutorial_Spinnell_FINAL.pdf)
- **Full-depth imbalance predicts edge stability (Columbia)**: favourable full-depth book imbalance had Spearman ρ ≈ 0.29 (p ≈ 9e-6, n = 230) with the change in edge over the next ~1 s or booking period. Top-5 and top-10 imbalance were not significant. A frozen logistic/Huber model (AUC 0.957 out of sample) still did not beat the simple rule economically: it lost $3,791 by rejecting one 4.42¢ trade. — [Coulter PDF §2.2, §2.5, §2.7](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Nathaniel%20Coulter%20lt3_postmortem_report.pdf)
- **Booking-period drift was positive on average**: arrival-book edge was $57,119, the refreshed book at booking implied $69,828, and realized was $70,185, so booking movement contributed +$12,709. One TAME BUY arrived with 5.18¢ edge and full-depth imbalance of −0.227, gained 12.78¢ during booking, and realized $12,033. — [same, §4.4](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Nathaniel%20Coulter%20lt3_postmortem_report.pdf)
- **Booking latency and fines**: hedging on the HTTP accept response, before the position was booked, produced $81,400 of fines on $30,512 of economic profit (displayed P&L −$50,888). The booking delay was 950 ms mean (927-977 ms 5th-95th percentile). — [same, §2.4-2.5](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Nathaniel%20Coulter%20lt3_postmortem_report.pdf) *(already in our docs/OFFICIAL_RULES.md)*
- **Acceptance timing**: raye92 `Ray.py` re-evaluates every tick and accepts at the first tick the edge clears cost. `Declan.py` needs 3 consecutive qualifying ticks. Helen LT4 and the Babson tutorial both say to use the 30 s window to wait for a better book. — [Ray.py](https://github.com/raye92/ritc-trading/blob/main/competition2/Liquidity/Ray.py); [Declan.py](https://github.com/raye92/ritc-trading/blob/main/competition2/Liquidity/Declan.py); [Babson](https://www.babson.edu/media/babson/assets/cutler-center/Rotman-Trading_LT4-Case-Tutorial_Spinnell_FINAL.pdf)
- **Late auction submission**: Felix 2023 submits at `expiration−1`, raye92 2026 at `expires−1` or `expires−2`, and RPI 2023 evaluates only when fewer than 15 ticks remain. — [Felix main.py](https://github.com/felixpoirier1/RIT/blob/main/Liquidity/main.py); [Ray.py](https://github.com/raye92/ritc-trading/blob/main/competition2/Liquidity/Ray.py); [RPI](https://github.com/JackMansfield2019/2023-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Liquidity_Case/Main_Liquidity.py)
- **Offsetting against existing inventory**: RPI's model splits a tender into `shares_to_sell_instantly` (the part that offsets the current position) and `shares_to_be_shorted`. The offsetting part is valued against the position's VWAP at no market impact; only the remainder is walked through the book. — [RPI Main_Liquidity.py](https://github.com/JackMansfield2019/2023-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Liquidity_Case/Main_Liquidity.py)
- **Close-out rules by year**:
  - 2023: open positions are closed at the last traded price, and "computerized market makers will increase the liquidity… towards the end… to ensure the closing price cannot be manipulated". Scoring used Adjusted P&L = tender P&L + min(0, speculation P&L). — [RITC 2023 package](https://github.com/LiChiLin/Rotman-Trading-Competition-2024/blob/main/2023%20Rotman%20(Prev%20Case)/ritc-2023-case-package.pdf)
  - 2026: the same close-out text, but real-time fines of $1/sh for the first 5,000 flagged speculative or front-running shares, $2/sh beyond that, and **$10/sh for shares uncovered at the end of the iteration**. — [RITC 2026 Case Package](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RITC%202026%20Case%20Package.pdf)
  - LT3 instructor default parameters: $0.20 / 5,000 / $0.40, and $1.00/sh for uncovered shares. — [LT3 SOLUTION](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RIT%20-%20Case%20Brief%20-%20LT3%20-%20Dynamic%20Order%20Arrival%20-%20SOLUTION.pdf)

### Inferences
- (Inferred) Our setup has no close-out fine and runs about 11 tenders per heat. Holding into the bell is a free option with zero expected value under a random walk, plus variance. A leader at +$200k vs our +$40k is consistent with a high-variance holder getting a good draw. The Columbia class spread (−$137k to +$106k with 32 traders) shows how wide that variance is. The scoring is rank-based per sub-heat (Q3), so variance is not free: one +$200k heat counts the same as any other 1st place.
- (Inferred) Columbia's +$12.7k booking-period gain and the Babson "unwind in 10-20 s" norm both point the same way. In the LT3 family, **liquidity at the touch tends to stay for a second or two after a tender arrives**, and fast crossing captures it. If our iceberg/AC unwind takes ~30 s, we may give up part of the edge when the book drifts back. (This is not tested against our simulator's refill model.)
- (Inferred) Columbia's best published edge signal (full-depth imbalance, ρ ≈ 0.29) is a candidate gate, but its own V6 model showed it did not add economic value at n ≈ 45. It probably fails our paired-t ≥ 2 rule too.

### Gaps
- No source says whether WTA reserve prices relate to mid or to the tender's private-offer price.
- No source documents using the RIT client tender UI in a way the API cannot.
- No source reports profit from auctions vs private tenders separately.

## Q3. What do official RIT/FRTL briefs, solutions, packages and "Closing Thoughts" say about intended play, scoring and common mistakes?

### Takeaway
The intended play is to **price liquidity, not direction**: walk the book for the full block, decline when depth or spread is insufficient, then mix market orders (to cut market risk) with limit orders (to cut impact). Scoring is **rank-based per sub-heat**, built to punish "betting the house". The 2026 director said calibration "worked well" and called this the "bread and butter" case, and hinted that some cases may be traded more manually in future.

### Cited Findings
- **Worked examples**. Accept: 50k CRZY at $9.90 against bids of 8k@9.98, 25k@9.95, 7k@9.90 and more below; 40k can be sold immediately at or above $9.90. Decline: the same block at $9.97, where only 8k is bid above the price. Market orders reduce market risk, large market orders cause impact, and "a successful liability trader" mixes market, limit and marketable-limit orders. — [LT3 SOLUTION brief](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RIT%20-%20Case%20Brief%20-%20LT3%20-%20Dynamic%20Order%20Arrival%20-%20SOLUTION.pdf); [RITCx Liability Trading Case](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RITCx-Liability%20Trading%20Case.pdf)
- **Book imbalance** may be used as a *liquidity* forecast (more buyers implies small impact for a sell). The brief warns that participants may "game" the book with fake orders, and that liquidity forecasting must not become price forecasting. — [same](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RIT%20-%20Case%20Brief%20-%20LT3%20-%20Dynamic%20Order%20Arrival%20-%20SOLUTION.pdf)
- **Volatility**: LT3 stocks are expected to move 10-15% within the 5-minute run (one simulated week). — [same](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RIT%20-%20Case%20Brief%20-%20LT3%20-%20Dynamic%20Order%20Arrival%20-%20SOLUTION.pdf)
- **Instructor grading** (5 tenders, 300 s): over $50k Very Good; over $35k Good; over $10k Average; over $0 Needs improvement; below $0 "Improper execution". — [same](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RIT%20-%20Case%20Brief%20-%20LT3%20-%20Dynamic%20Order%20Arrival%20-%20SOLUTION.pdf)
- **RITC 2026 structure**: 2 heats × 5 sub-heats of 600 s, each with different stocks (RITC/COMP; TRNT/MTRL; BLU/RED/GRN; WDY/BZZ/BNN; VNS/MRS/JPTR/STRN). Fees are $0.01-$0.04. Tender windows are 30 s, then 20 s in sub-heats 4-5. Limits are 250k gross / 150k net, max order 10k, no commission on tenders. "Every participant will receive the same number of tender offers and face comparable profit potential". — [RITC 2026 Case Package](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RITC%202026%20Case%20Package.pdf)
- **Auction / WTA rules (2023 and 2026, identical wording)**. Competitive auction: any bid better than the hidden reserve fills at the bidder's own price, regardless of other bids. WTA: the single best bid wins if it meets the hidden reserve, otherwise nobody wins. — [RITC 2026 Case Package](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RITC%202026%20Case%20Package.pdf); [RITC 2023 package](https://github.com/LiChiLin/Rotman-Trading-Competition-2024/blob/main/2023%20Rotman%20(Prev%20Case)/ritc-2023-case-package.pdf)
- **Scoring (2026)**: within each sub-heat, teams are ranked by summed team P&L (adjusted for penalties). The case rank is the average of sub-heat ranks, mapped to n+1 points. The system is "designed to discourage participants from 'betting the house' in one sub-heat" and rewards consistency: placing 8th, 5th and 10th beats placing 1st, 10th and 35th. The Liquidity Risk case is 20% of the total (25% in 2023). — [RITC 2026 Case Package, Scoring Methodology](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RITC%202026%20Case%20Package.pdf); [RITC 2023 package](https://github.com/LiChiLin/Rotman-Trading-Competition-2024/blob/main/2023%20Rotman%20(Prev%20Case)/ritc-2023-case-package.pdf)
- **Speculation scoring (2019-2023)**: Adjusted P&L = P/L from tenders + min(0, P/L from speculation). For example, $10k from tenders plus $50k from speculation scores $10k. A "Performance Evaluation Tool" was provided. — [RITC 2023 package](https://github.com/LiChiLin/Rotman-Trading-Competition-2024/blob/main/2023%20Rotman%20(Prev%20Case)/ritc-2023-case-package.pdf)
- **2026 Closing Thoughts (Eric Kang, Director)**: the Liquidity Risk Case "continues to be our 'bread and butter' case… a great introduction to the RIT platform"; "calibrations and parameterization worked well this year". 2026 allowed the DMA API alongside REST. Organizers will consider "whether there may still be value in certain cases being traded in a more manual or traditional fashion rather than through fully automated approaches". — [RITC 2026 Closing Thoughts](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RITC%202026-Closing%20Thoughts.pdf)
- **Official support scripts** (LT3 Python support; RITCx LT3 DMA support) only print the cumulative-volume and cumulative-VWAP book view, plus pending tenders, "to judge whether the book has enough liquidity to unwind a tender at a profit before you accept it". — [RITCx LT3 support script](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/main/RITCx%20LT3%20Case%20support%20script-DMA%20API.py)

### Inferences
- (Inferred) Under rank scoring, the target is consistently high rank per heat, not maximum dollars. Matching a ~$200k practice leader matters only if that leader's approach also ranks well in most heats. The practice heat's leader may be a high-variance holder who would rank poorly in other draws.
- (Inferred) The official close-out text has been the same since 2023: last traded price, with market makers adding liquidity near the end. That supports our A16 assumption (close ≈ mid). The 2026 $10/sh uncovered fine is the key precedent against holding; our setup lacks it.

### Gaps
- No official "solution" for auction/WTA bidding was found. The LT3 SOLUTION file covers private tenders only.
- No official statement on the typical reserve-to-mid distance.

## Q4. Post-mortems, blogs, LinkedIn/Medium, YouTube, Reddit by participants

### Takeaway
Only one real post-mortem exists (Columbia 2026, classroom LT3) plus one practitioner tutorial (Babson LT4). The rest is code. Web searches across Medium, LinkedIn, Reddit and YouTube returned no Liquidity Risk / Liability Trading write-ups.

### Cited Findings
- Columbia post-mortem. Main lessons: (1) "Top-of-book spread is not block edge": 30 of 35 class tenders looked profitable at the best quote after fees, only 14 of 35 after a full-block sweep. The 16 false positives averaged +16.19¢ at the quote but −15.99¢ for the full block. (2) Blindly accepting all 23 fully coverable tenders would have been worth about −$75,623, against +$57,119 for the selected 8. (3) The binding constraint is the ~1 s booking floor. (4) Simple rules beat the ML model. — [Coulter PDF §4.3, §6](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Nathaniel%20Coulter%20lt3_postmortem_report.pdf)
- Columbia: the class opportunity set was much poorer than demo runs. Acceptance rates were 22.9% in class vs 44-74% in demos. 21 of 35 tenders had negative full-block edge, and 12 of 35 lacked depth for the full block. — [same, §4.2](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/main/Nathaniel%20Coulter%20lt3_postmortem_report.pdf)
- Babson LT4: "The most common way people lose money is due to low liquidity" combined with a slow unwind while the market moves against you. "Speed is very important in this case". After each P&L spike, the line goes flat within 10-20 s. — [Babson LT4 tutorial](https://www.babson.edu/media/babson/assets/cutler-center/Rotman-Trading_LT4-Case-Tutorial_Spinnell_FINAL.pdf)
- From 2024, RITC packaged tender-liquidity risk inside an ETF case: joshlim01 describes 2024's "ETF Case" as one where teams "assess liquidity risk across tender offers". RITC 2025 repos (QinqinAndMacaulayCat) do "tender arbitrage" on ETF/stock conversion. These versions are not directly comparable to the CRZY/TAME stock case. — [joshlim01/RITC2024 README](https://github.com/joshlim01/RITC2024/blob/main/README.md); [QinqinAndMacaulayCat/RITC2025 README](https://github.com/QinqinAndMacaulayCat/RITC2025/blob/main/README.md)
- U Calgary finished 3rd overall at RITC 2023 (43 schools) and 2024 (42 schools); both years included the Liquidity Risk case. Per-case ranks were not given. — [UCalgary RITC results](https://haskayne.ucalgary.ca/labs/finance-trading/trading-software/competitions/ritc-results)

### Inferences
- (Inferred) What competitors broadly do that we do not: (1) **market-order unwinds that finish in 0.3-20 s**, vs our ~30 s passive iceberg/AC; (2) **auction bids anchored to the touch plus a small buffer, submitted at expiry**, vs our break-even − $0.05; (3) in manual play, **partial discretionary holding** when the book is lopsided in your favour. Only (1) can be tested without speculation-fine risk, and it is the cheapest A/B to run: cross immediately after booking with a full-block marketable-limit sweep vs the current iceberg/AC.
- (Inferred) No source shows that a disciplined algorithm reaches $150-200k per heat in an LT3-family case. The documented figures are: Columbia demo average of roughly $27k per 5-tender heat (V5: $187k over 34 tenders; heat count inferred as ~7); Babson "near-perfect" manual at ~$75k; LT3 "Very Good" at over $50k. The $200k practice leader is most plausibly variance from holding plus auction wins (unverified).
