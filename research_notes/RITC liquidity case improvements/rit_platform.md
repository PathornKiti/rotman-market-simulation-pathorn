# RIT platform mechanics for the Liquidity Risk / Liability Trading (LT3) case: tenders, auctions, computer market makers, end-of-case liquidity

Scope: what the Rotman Interactive Trader (RIT) itself does, as documented by Rotman or reported by other teams, and what each mechanic implies for the `liability` bot. Labels used in every bullet: **[DOC]** = official Rotman document or API spec; **[COMMUNITY]** = another team's code, logs or notes (real-server observation, not official); **[OURS]** = our own practice-heat logs; inferences are only in the "Inferences" subsections.

Files fetched for this note (copies in the session scratchpad, not in the repo): both swagger specs, the RotmanFRTL PDFs/scripts, and the GitHub files cited below.

## 1. How RIT generates tender offers (types, price vs market, reserve, ids, timing, captions)

### Takeaway
Rotman documents only the rules, not the generator: private tender prices are "influenced by the current market price" and always carry a "favourable spread". Auction and winner-take-all (WTA) awards are set against a hidden reserve. In the closest public real-server dataset (the 2026 class LT3 casefile), private tenders came at a **median $0.205/share better than the mid** (range −$0.04 to +$0.31). Ids are pre-allocated slots, not a running counter. Each tender type has its own caption wording, which other teams used to classify tenders.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] 2027 brief: private tenders let you buy or sell a fixed volume "at a price influenced by the current market price", executed "immediately from the participant's inventory". Competitive auction: "Bids that exceed a hidden baseline reserve price are automatically filled, regardless of competing bids … the transaction executes at the price submitted by the participant." WTA: "awarded to the highest bid (or lowest offer) that meets the hidden baseline reserve price. If no submission meets the reserve, the tender is void." Every participant "receives an equal number of tenders with variations in price and quantity", and different participants "may receive different offers at different times". — local `document/Liquidity Risk Case - for selection 2027.pdf`
- [DOC] RITC 2026 package, same case: "Tender offers will be generated by computerized traders and distributed at random intervals to random participants." For WTA, the award comes "After all prices have been received". Every participant gets "the same number of tender offers and face comparable profit potential, although offer prices and quantities may vary." — [RITC 2026 Case Package.pdf](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RITC%202026%20Case%20Package.pdf)
- [DOC] LT3 instructor notes (case build 1.08): in LT3, tender "size, price, and frequency … are drawn from a random distribution each time the case is run". The orders are "to buy shares from you at a premium to the market price, or sell shares to you at a discount to the market price", and "all institutional orders will have a favourable spread associated with them". — [RIT - Case Brief - LT3 - Dynamic Order Arrival - SOLUTION.pdf](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RIT%20-%20Case%20Brief%20-%20LT3%20-%20Dynamic%20Order%20Arrival%20-%20SOLUTION.pdf); same wording in [RITCx-Liability Trading Case.pdf](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RITCx-Liability%20Trading%20Case.pdf)
- [DOC] The official private-tender caption template: "An institution would like to SELL 75,000 shares of CRZY to you at a price of $9.75. Would you like to BUY the shares from them?" — LT3 SOLUTION brief (above)
- [COMMUNITY] Real caption from a 2026 practice server (ETF case): `An institution would like to SELL 85000 shares of RITC to you at a price of 24.89.  Would you like to BUY the shares from them?`. `action` is from the trader's side (BUY = we buy), and the ticker parses reliably from the caption. — [jdishihara heat_observations.md](https://github.com/jdishihara/market-sim-trading-engine/blob/HEAD/arb_case/notes/heat_observations.md)
- [COMMUNITY] A 2023 RITC Liquidity bot sorts tenders by caption keyword: `'institution'` → private, `'winner'` → WTA, `'reserve'` → competitive auction. So the auction and WTA captions contain "reserve" and "winner" respectively. The same bot bids non-fixed tenders only once fewer than 15 ticks remain. — [JackMansfield2019 Main_Liquidity.py, `get_type_of_tender` and main loop](https://github.com/JackMansfield2019/2023-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/HEAD/Liquidity_Case/Main_Liquidity.py)
- [COMMUNITY] Another RITC Liquidity bot bids auctions only at `tick == expiration-1`, at the book VWAP for the full size ±0.1%. — [felixpoirier1 RIT Liquidity/main.py](https://github.com/felixpoirier1/RIT/blob/HEAD/Liquidity/main.py)
- [COMMUNITY] 2026 LT3 class casefile, 7 heats, 35 private tenders (Columbia team CSV; my computation from the `tender_price`/`midpoint` columns, edge = in our favour vs mid):
  - Edge: median **+$0.205/share**, mean +$0.187, sd $0.087, min −$0.04, max +$0.31. 4 of 35 were under $0.05.
  - Size: 65,000-75,000 shares. Windows: `expires − tick` = 30 or 31 ticks.
  - Arrivals: about ticks 15-27, 77-90, 137-149, 197-208 and 257-270, i.e. about every 60 s with ±10 s jitter.
  - **Tender ids were the same in every heat: 49, 849, 1649, 2449, 3249** (step 800).
  — [lt3_competition_tenders.csv](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/HEAD/LT3/data%20and%20logs%20(from%20the%20competition)/lt3_competition_tenders.csv)
- [COMMUNITY] ETF-case tenders on a 2026 practice server ran +0.15, +0.12, −0.19, +0.48, −0.14, +0.48, +0.05, +0.26 vs fair, so RIT can generate tenders priced *against* the trader. They were 71-95k shares, one every ~30 ticks, alternating BUY/SELL. — [Eka416 ETF_NOTES.md](https://github.com/Eka416/Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/HEAD/etf/ETF_NOTES.md)
- [OURS] 2026 practice server, this case:
  - 11 tenders/heat, mostly 50-59k shares, windows 30-31 ticks.
  - Fixed ids 3, 103, …, 603 (step 100); auction ids 700-703 with `price = None`.
  - Accepted fixed tenders were +$0.02 to +$0.78/share vs the mid at answer time.
  - Both auction wins were id 702.
  — local `docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md` §1b, `docs/PRACTICE_LOGS.md` §2-3

### Inferences
- Ids are fixed per heat (49+800k in the class file, 3+100k and 700-703 for us), so the casefile probably pre-schedules a list of tender "slots". Each id is likely a scheduled row in the server casefile, not a counter. Two consequences:
  1. **The id identifies the tender's row and type.** Combined with our 702-wins pattern, the rows 700-703 probably alternate between the competitive and WTA types, or carry different reserve rules. Reading the `caption` ("reserve" vs "winner") decides it with no guesswork. The bot should log and branch on the caption (bid at the reserve-beating price for competitive; bid against rivals for WTA).
  2. The arrival schedule and sizes are drawn per run, but the number and order of slots are fixed. Expect exactly 7 private + 4 auction slots per heat on this server, and plan net-limit room around the 4 auction slots.
- Private edges in the class casefile cluster near $0.20 ± 0.09. Our server's accepted range (+0.02 to +0.78) is wider, which fits a casefile with a larger premium distribution and higher vol. Both are consistent with "price = mid ± U-shaped or normal premium". **None was strongly negative in LT3**, unlike the ETF case, so a tender far below its own premium distribution most likely means the book has moved since the price was set. The price is fixed at arrival (A6 confirmed), and the mid moves during the 30 s window.
- The official solution's "decline" example (a block priced $0.03 better than the touch, with the book unable to absorb it) shows Rotman's intended rule: judge the edge against *walkable liquidity*, not the mid.

### Gaps
- No Rotman document gives the premium distribution, the reserve rule (fixed offset from the mid? from the tender's own price?), or whether reserves differ between competitive and WTA slots. The server casefile (`.xlsx`) is distributed only to instructors; I found no public copy.
- I found no public example of the auction or WTA caption text. The keywords "reserve" and "winner" are inferred from a 2023 bot's classifier, not a recorded caption. Our local journals with captions come from our own simulator (`Client wants you to BUY 30000 CROC`), not the real server.
- Whether private tenders in a given slot go to all teams at once (a crowd risk) is not documented. The brief's "different times" suggests not.

## 2. Tender API mechanics: fields, accepting vs bidding, re-bidding, award timing, room checks, errors

### Takeaway
The API spec lets a trader bid on a non-fixed tender with `POST /tenders/{id}?price=` and returns only `{success: bool}`. Nothing documents re-bidding, and the award is not reported in the response. RIT refuses a tender transaction when the trader lacks limit room at the moment it executes ("You have won the offer … but you have insufficient … trade limits"). A won auction therefore needs room at award time, not at bid time.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] Client REST 1.0.3, `GET /tenders` fields: `tender_id, period, tick, expires, caption, quantity, action (BUY/SELL), is_fixed_bid, price`, with `price` "non-null if `is_fixed_bid` is `true`". (The spec omits `ticker`, but real servers return it: the official template and our logs use `tender['ticker']`.) `POST /tenders/{id}` = "Accept the tender", with `price` "Required if the tender is not fixed-bid". `DELETE` = decline. Both return `SuccessResult {success: boolean}`. — [REST swagger.yaml](https://rit.306w.ca/RIT-REST-API/1.0.3/swagger.yaml)
- [DOC] DMA 1.0.5 makes `price` **required** on every accept: "Bid price for the tender. If tender is fixed-bid, this value must match the tender price." — [DMA swagger.yaml](https://rit.306w.ca/RIT-DMA-API/1.0.5/swagger.yaml)
- [DOC] Errors: HTTP 200 on success, 400-500 on failure, 401 on a bad key, 429 + `Retry-After` header + `wait` body on rate limits. Error body `{code, message}`; DMA's example code is `RATE_LIMIT_EXCEEDED`. — both swagger specs
- [DOC] RTD `TENDERINFO` returns "ID, Ticker, Volume, Price, tick at which the offer was received, tick at which the offer will expire" (example `11,TAME,58000,24.51,49,79`: a 30-tick window). "In the RIT, each tender offer has a unique ID during an iteration of any case." — local `document/RIT - RTD_Documentation.pdf` p.5
- [DOC] UI guide: a private tender, if accepted, settles at quantity × price (example 78,000 × $10.39 = $810,420). — [RIT User Application (RIT Client) Feature Guide.pdf](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RIT%20User%20Application%20(RIT%20Client)%20Feature%20Guide.pdf) p.11-12
- [DOC/organiser Q&A, RIT-based RETC competition] Error text seen by a trader: "You have won the offer to buy RITC at 24.89, but you have insufficient positions, containments or trade limits." Organiser answer: "Any transaction will be constrained by the position limit … If you do not have sufficient 'room' in your position, you will not be able to receive a tender position." — [LUISS RETC 2022 technical Q&A](https://retc.luiss.it/qas/2022-qas/technical/); [2021 Q&A](https://retc.luiss.it/qas/2021-qas/technical/)
- [DOC] Order rate cap on that RIT deployment: "the maximum is ten orders per second". This is a per-case setting, not a constant. — LUISS RETC Q&A (above)
- [COMMUNITY] Booking delay: accept → position visible in `/securities` took 961-1046 ms on 7 accepts (2026 LT3 class server). — [Columbia Terminal Outputs 9_22_2026.txt](https://github.com/Nathaniel-Coulter/Columbia-Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/HEAD/LT3/data%20and%20logs%20(from%20the%20competition)/Terminal%20Outputs%209_22_2026.txt). [OURS] 0.8-1.1 s — local `docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md` A9
- [OURS] 18 auction bids, 0 rejected at submission, 2 won (booked after the window); re-bidding untested. — local `docs/RESEARCH.md` "Auction clues"
- [COMMUNITY] Teams that bid auctions do it late, at `expires−1` or with fewer than 15 ticks left. None of the reviewed code re-POSTs a bid. — felixpoirier1, JackMansfield2019 (links in §1)

### Inferences
- **Limit room is checked at award time.** The RETC message pairs "You have won" with "insufficient … limits", and the organisers say you "will not be able to receive a tender position" without room. So on RIT a won competitive or WTA tender that does not fit at award is simply not delivered. This is decision-relevant: our bot holds net-limit room for a pending auction bid, and that blocked fixed tenders in 2 of 5 heats (≈$15k when it happens). If an over-limit award just fails, with no fine (none is documented in our brief for this; the RETC $0.50 fine was for converter-caused breaches), then **filling the room with a fixed tender is free, and the auction bid becomes a costless option that only executes if room is left at expiry.** Test it in one practice heat: bid an auction, take a same-direction fixed tender that leaves no room, and watch the Transaction Log at expiry.
- Re-bidding: the spec models accept as an idempotent "Accept the tender" with a price, and the award is decided at expiry. The likely server behaviour is that a second POST either replaces the stored price or is rejected. Both are cheap to test: POST a far-off price early, then the real price at `expires−2`, and see whether the win books at the second price. If replacement works, the safe pattern is an early placeholder bid as insurance against a missed late POST, then refine near expiry. Since the award is at expiry, there is no information cost to bidding late either way.
- `success: true` on a bid means only "bid stored", never "won" (consistent with 0/18 rejections). The award is visible only through a position change after `expires` (plus ~1 s booking) or a `TENDER` row in the DMA `/transactions` log (see §5).

### Gaps
- No documentation of re-bid behaviour, of the exact award tick, or of whether WTA ties are broken by time.
- No source on what error code or message RIT returns for a stale or expired tender id, or for a price that does not match on a fixed tender.

## 3. Computer traders and market makers: quoting, refill, price impact, end-of-case liquidity

### Takeaway
Rotman describes the market as order-driven: prices clear on the orders of students *and* a programmable "AI Order Flow" (anonymous `ANON` traders). Price impact is therefore real and endogenous. The lab's own teaching material describes books that refill ("resiliency … re-fill rate … time for the price to recover"), and community measurements show books that refill every tick. How large or fast the end-of-case liquidity boost is, and whether impact is permanent, is not published.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] "market clearing prices are determined and recorded at millisecond frequencies, based on orders from market participants: both students and the AI Order Flow. The AI Order Flow can be switched off or programmed to achieve specific learning objectives. Market clearing, based on actions of all the participants … ensures students are not exogenous to the market … price impact due to liquidity issues … can be experienced." — [Rotman RIT Market Simulator overview](https://www.rotman.utoronto.ca/faculty-and-research/education-labs/bmo-financial-group-finance-research-and-trading-lab/rit-market-simulator/overview--features/)
- [DOC] Close-out: "All open positions are closed at the end of the trading case based on the last traded price. Computerized market makers increase liquidity near the end of trading to ensure closing prices cannot be easily manipulated." — local 2027 brief; same text in the RITC 2026 package
- [DOC] The FRTL's definition of resiliency for RIT cases: "Resiliency can be measured by the re-fill rate, that is, how quickly new limit orders are submitted to the visible book when liquidity is removed from the book. Resiliency measures the time for the price to recover after a large trade." It suggests a Market Efficiency Coefficient MEC = var(long-window returns)/(k·var(short-window returns)), with 30 s vs 6 s windows in RIT; MEC < 1 indicates an illiquid market. — [ALGO3 Smart Order Routing SOLUTION.pdf](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RIT%20-%20Case%20Brief%20-%20ALGO3%20-%20Smart%20Order%20Routing%20-%20SOLUTION.pdf)
- [DOC] Book entries from computer traders show as `ANON` (UI example: "Broker ANON submitted a bid to buy 11,200 shares of CRZY at $10.00"). Other cases describe "programmed order flow (the ANON liquidity traders)" that vary the spread "across securities and over time". — Feature Guide p.5; [RITCx MM Capstone brief](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RITCx-Algorithmic%20Market%20Making%20Captone%20Case.pdf)
- [DOC] LT3 solution: book imbalance signals liquidity on one side, but "the picture becomes very muddled when/if traders begin to 'game' the information system", and stocks are "expected to move as much as 10-15%" over a 5-minute heat. — LT3 SOLUTION brief
- [COMMUNITY] Books on a 2026 practice server (ETF case) are built from individual ~10,000-share `ANON` orders, about 10k at the best level and about 30k in the top 3. Walking a 100k block cost 19¢ at the median and 40¢ at worst. The server's `dry_run` VWAP matched a local book walk exactly, so **a market order fills against the visible book only; there is no hidden liquidity at the instant of execution**. Traded volume: median 17,900 shares/tick. — [jdishihara heat_observations.md](https://github.com/jdishihara/market-sim-trading-engine/blob/HEAD/arb_case/notes/heat_observations.md)
- [COMMUNITY] "Walking the books for the whole 80-95k block at once is never viable (0.1 to 0.8 per share of impact) … but the books refill each tick, so the realistic exit is to work the block at the touch over ~20 ticks." Also, "the actual RITC touch over the 20 ticks after each tender varied a lot", which argues for hedging quickly. — [Eka416 ETF_NOTES.md](https://github.com/Eka416/Rotman-Trading-Competition/blob/HEAD/etf/ETF_NOTES.md)
- [COMMUNITY] 2026 LT3 class server: a full 65-75k block hedged at market in a 35-110 ms burst had realised minus modelled (visible-book walk) error of −$0.13 to +$0.04/share, and P&L of $11-20k per block. So the visible book was a fair predictor of an instant market sweep. — Columbia Terminal Outputs (link in §2)
- [COMMUNITY] A model that walks the visible book was pessimistic by about +$0.07/share against realised unwinds (passive fills at the touch, plus rebates). — jdishihara heat_observations.md
- [OURS] On our server, resting at the touch absorbed 50k blocks at about 2,500-3,600 shares/s with little price movement. The two slow unwinds (701 and 893 shares/s) came with −$1.76 and −$0.83 adverse moves. The book-walk model undervalued accepted tenders by about $0.19/share. — local `docs/PRACTICE_LOGS.md` §4

### Inferences
- Taken together: (a) an instant sweep pays exactly the visible-book walk; (b) the book refills on a ~1-tick scale; (c) the price follows its own random walk on top. So **temporary impact recovers through refill, while the permanent component is not documented**. Our resting-unwind data (rates of 2.5-3.6k shares/s without trend) fit mostly transient impact on this server. The Rotman description ("not exogenous to the market") means a crowd of teams selling the same auction block *will* move the price. This supports keeping a crowd guard for auctions and WTA, which go to everyone, and not for private tenders.
- The FRTL's own MEC (30 s vs 6 s variance ratio) is a cheap live resiliency gauge the organisers expect teams to use. MEC well below 1 on a stock means slow refill, which is the regime that produced both of our big losses. It could gate how long we rest before crossing. We already compute a variance ratio in `ritc calibrate`, so this needs no new modelling, only a live per-stock flag.
- End-of-case liquidity: Rotman says the computer market makers "increase liquidity near the end". That is the best time to unwind any remaining position at low impact, and it makes the last traded price hard to push. Holding into the bell (A15) relies on this, but see §4: the default casefile fines uncovered shares.

### Gaps
- No published numbers on when the end-of-case liquidity boost starts, how deep the market makers quote, their spread, or how fast they refill. These must come from our own recordings (depth by third of the heat, last 30 s), as planned in ASSUMPTIONS B3.
- No source states whether the AI Order Flow reacts to *our* orders (for example, momentum-following after a sweep) or how much impact is permanent versus transient. A recorder test would answer it: sweep 10k, then track the mid for 10-30 s against a matched no-trade baseline.

## 4. End-of-case close-out and the case fines module (decision-critical for A15)

### Takeaway
RIT's `LIABILITY_RISK` fines module has a **fourth parameter, a $/share fine on shares "not covered by the end of the case iteration"**, defaulting to **$1.00** in the stock LT3 casefile ($10 in RITC 2026). The 2027 brief lists only the speculation tiers. The organisers raised tier 3 to $0.60, which shows they are editing this module, so parameter 4 may well be non-zero. Holding inventory to the bell, as the bot deliberately does, is exposed to this fine.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] LT3 instructor notes: "The 'LIABILITY_RISK' case fines module (in the 'Modules' sheet in the server casefile) includes four parameters. Parameter 1 sets the $ per share fine rate associated with front-running and speculative strategies up to a threshold of the number of shares set by Parameter 2. For shares in excess of that threshold, the fine rate is increased to that specified as Parameter 3. Finally, Parameter 4 sets the fine rate for shares not covered by the end of the case iteration. Note that the threshold number of shares (Parameter 2) applies to the entire iteration and aggregated for all stocks. The values for these parameters are set at $0.20, 5000, $0.40 and $1.00." Fines appear in the transaction log, in a "Case Fines Summary" in the end-of-iteration report, and in "Total Fines" in Trader Info. — [LT3 SOLUTION brief p.5](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RIT%20-%20Case%20Brief%20-%20LT3%20-%20Dynamic%20Order%20Arrival%20-%20SOLUTION.pdf)
- [DOC] The student brief defines speculative trading as "trading unrelated to unwinding accepted tender offers **and any nonzero positions at the end of the trading period**". — LT3 SOLUTION brief; RITCx Liability Trading Case brief
- [DOC] RITC 2026 used $1 / 5,000 / $2 / **$10 per uncovered share**, "aggregated across all stocks for the entire iteration", applied "real-time in RIT". — [RITC 2026 Case Package.pdf](https://github.com/RotmanFRTL/RotmanFRTL.github.io/blob/HEAD/RITC%202026%20Case%20Package.pdf)
- [DOC] 2027 brief: $0.20 for the first 5,000 shares and $0.40 beyond, plus "All open positions are closed at the end … based on the last traded price". It names no uncovered-share fine. The organisers have since raised tier 3 to $0.60. — local 2027 brief; local `docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md` §6 q0
- [DOC] The 2027 brief's first two tiers equal the stock LT3 defaults ($0.20 / 5,000 / $0.40). — compare the two sources above
- [DOC] DMA `/transactions` has transaction types `CASE_FINE`, `CLOSE_OUT`, `TENDER`, `COMMISSION`, `LIMIT_REBATE`, and `/trader` returns `total_fines`. The Client REST 1.0.3 spec has neither. — DMA vs REST swagger

### Inferences
- The 2027 brief reproduces the stock LT3 tiers 1-3 word for word, so it was almost certainly written from the stock casefile, and the stock casefile ships with parameter 4 = $1.00. Unless the organisers zeroed it, every share still open at the bell costs about $1.00 on top of the close-out. **At the bot's planned end-of-heat inventory (up to ~125k shares simulated) that is up to ~$125k, wiping out a heat.** Even at $1.00 it dwarfs the ~$0.02-0.05/share the bell hold is meant to save. Until a practice heat proves the fine is zero (a `CASE_FINE` row at the last tick in DMA `/transactions`, or Total Fines in Trader Info jumping at the bell), the decision-relevant default is the A15 fallback (`hold_risk_budget=0`, `close_hold_ticks=0`, `hold_valuation=false`).
- "Threshold applies to the entire iteration and aggregated for all stocks" means the cheap $0.20 tier is used up once across the whole heat. Every speculative share after the first 5,000 cumulative costs the top rate ($0.60 now). Small past-zero overshoots therefore add up across the heat, which supports the existing `order_settle` fix.
- **The DMA API can show fines live.** Polling `/trader.total_fines` and `/transactions?after=` for `CASE_FINE` rows would let the bot (a) confirm A10/A8/A15 in a practice heat automatically and (b) detect and stop a fine-generating behaviour within a second. The Client REST API lacks both endpoints. The project already supports DMA auth (`RIT_AUTH`).

### Gaps
- The 2027 parameter 4 value is not published. Only the Transaction Log or Trader Info of a practice heat that ends holding shares, or the organisers, can settle it.
- No source says whether parameter 4 counts all open shares or only "tender-related" ones. The 2026 wording is "tender-related exposure"; the casefile wording is "shares not covered".

## 5. Other documented API features worth using

### Takeaway
The spec documents three features the bot does not use:
- a server-side **`dry_run=1` market-order simulator** that returns the exact fill VWAP without trading;
- a per-order **`X-Wait-Until` header**;
- per-security `api_orders_per_second`, `execution_delay_ms`, `trading_fee` and `limit_order_rebate` fields.

Plus, on DMA only, live fines and a transaction log.

### Cited Findings
- [DOC] `POST /orders … dry_run (0/1)`: "Only available if `type` is `MARKET`. *Simulates* the order execution and returns the result as if the order was executed." On success the response carries the header `X-Wait-Until`: "Number of seconds to wait before attempting another order insertion against the same ticker". — [REST swagger.yaml](https://rit.306w.ca/RIT-REST-API/1.0.3/swagger.yaml); `dry_run` is also in DMA 1.0.5
- [COMMUNITY] `dry_run` checked live: it returned `order_id: null`, left positions unchanged, and matched a local book walk to 4 decimals. — [jdishihara heat_observations.md](https://github.com/jdishihara/market-sim-trading-engine/blob/HEAD/arb_case/notes/heat_observations.md)
- [DOC] `/securities` exposes `trading_fee`, `limit_order_rebate`, `max_trade_size`, `api_orders_per_second`, `execution_delay_ms`, `min_trade_size` and `quoted_decimals`. — REST swagger
- [COMMUNITY] Values on the 2026 LT3 class server: `apiRate=200` orders/s and `execDelayMs=0` for both CRZY and TAME, fee 0.02. On the 2026 ETF practice server: passive fills earned a **0.03 rebate** (the vendor script said 0.01; "read from the securities record"). — Columbia Terminal Outputs; Eka416 ETF_NOTES.md; jdishihara heat_observations.md
- [DOC] `POST /commands/cancel` accepts `all`, `ticker`, `ids` or a query string (e.g. `Ticker='CL' AND Price>124.23 AND Volume<0`). — REST swagger
- [DOC] REST: "most of the default RIT cases will have a very 'loose' rate-limiting"; securities may carry stricter per-security limits. DMA: "All endpoints are rate-limited … users of the DMA REST API are more likely to experience this response." — both swaggers
- [COMMUNITY] Book payload keys on real servers are `bids` / `asks`, not the spec's `bid` / `ask`; `quantity_filled` must be subtracted. — jdishihara heat_observations.md
- [COMMUNITY] Measured global capacity ~80 requests/s before 429s, with `wait` of 2-17 ms. — jdishihara heat_observations.md

### Inferences
- `dry_run` lets the bot ask the server what an instant sweep of N shares would cost, an exact oracle for tender pricing and the "cross the excess" step, rather than a local walk over a book snapshot that may be truncated or stale. It cannot price refill or resting fills, so it is a lower bound on exit value (the pessimistic side, which is where our auction bids lose; see PRACTICE_LOGS §3). A free check: assert dry_run VWAP = local walk once per stock at start-up, which also validates the book parser.
- Read `limit_order_rebate` and `trading_fee` at start-up (A17). If this server pays a passive rebate the way the ETF practice server did (0.03 vs a 0.02 fee), resting rather than crossing is worth 5¢/share. That is 25% of a median private-tender edge, and it shifts the hold/cross trade-off and the auction bid level.
- Use `X-Wait-Until` and `api_orders_per_second` from the server rather than fixed sleeps. Async booking (our duplicate-order fines) is a separate issue, but honouring `X-Wait-Until` avoids 429-induced retries that could also produce duplicates.
